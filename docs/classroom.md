# Mission Alive: 30-minute classroom activity

Audience: ages 10–14. Begin at `CREW BRIEFING` and treat the four training modules as one shared journey. `CREW CERTIFICATION` tracks the same four locally saved stamps. Each module places a three-beat story card and training brief before its challenge, then a first-person transmission after completion. The interactions illustrate ideas rather than reproduce mission physics.

## Learning objectives

Learners will debug a short rover program and distinguish habitability from proof of life; explain why a Mars aircraft needs onboard control; distinguish telescope deployment, alignment and infrared observing; and compare a cultural message with the time a radio signal takes to cross space.

## Before exploring — 5 minutes

Ask: How would you explore a place you cannot visit? What instructions would a machine need? Explain that the command blocks, energy costs, map, and animation speeds are made for learning, not actual rover software or telemetry.

## Explore in pairs — 12 minutes

Spend about three minutes per module; allow more time for a first run. One student builds Curiosity's program while the other predicts each move, then use Step to debug it together. Alternatively, complete the Free Explore science targets. After either route, choose the conclusion the rock evidence supports. On Ingenuity, choose the onboard controller, test the radio link, set rotor power in the 65–85% band and trim in the fixed 53–77 band, then attempt a flat-plain landing. Compare unsafe sites only if time permits. On Webb, open both wings, align all three images, then reveal the infrared view. On Voyager, play one original greeting and save a recorded or typed greeting. Record rotation and outward travel are optional; use the optional signal lab to compare one-way and round-trip delays.

## Discuss — 5 minutes

What did your action change? Which trade-off felt most difficult? What would you record to represent life on Earth? Distinguish a source-backed fact from a design choice in the prototype.

## Quiz — 5 minutes

1. Why does Curiosity's program need a loop block in this simulation?
2. Does evidence that Mars could support microbes prove that life existed there?
3. Why might engineers begin with a small flight demonstration?
4. How many flights did Ingenuity complete?
5. What is the difference between unfolding Webb and aligning its mirrors?
6. How many primary mirror segments does Webb have?
7. What kinds of things does the Golden Record carry?
8. Is Voyager's live distance estimate direct spacecraft telemetry? What happens when mission data is unavailable?

## Answer key and closing — 3 minutes

1. The shortest direct solution needs 12 primitive instructions, while the program is limited to 11 blocks; a loop compresses repeated movement.
2. No. Habitability is not proof of life.
3. To test whether a new approach works before committing to a larger system.
4. 72; see the NASA source linked in Ingenuity's fact card.
5. Unfolding opens the structure; alignment makes the segments work together optically.
6. 18; see NASA's Webb mirror reference.
7. Sounds, images, music, and greetings from Earth; see NASA's Golden Record overview.
8. No. The live display estimates range using JPL Horizons data and advances it between updates. The dated NASA projection remains an offline fallback; neither is direct spacecraft telemetry.

At four stamps, read the graduation message together and ask each learner to retell one machine's story to a partner. The existing certificate accepts a name and prints the date and four stamps; no additional mission patch is needed.

## Access notes

Use keyboard Tab and Enter/Space for buttons; arrow keys and Home/End for sliders. Curiosity provides command palette, program reorder/remove, Step and Run controls. Ingenuity's trim target stays fixed. Voyager offers 55 greeting recordings with language search, a country/region filter, audio controls and NASA's published English translations. After listening, `Type your greeting instead` provides a microphone-free completion path; audio recording is also available after permission. Voyager's polite announcement region reports playback, saved greetings and its earned stamp. These supports require browser and assistive-technology testing; their presence alone is not a claim of a completed screen-reader audit. Choose reduced motion or 2D view when useful.

Personal greetings stay on that browser unless explicitly shared. The optional public audio archive requires consent and moderation; classroom records also require teacher approval before submissions become public. Establish classroom consent and deletion rules before sharing. Names entered on the existing printable certificate stay local. Initial loading, recordings not yet loaded and live mission data require network access. Offline reload is not guaranteed.
